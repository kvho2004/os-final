Anushka Reflection
This project gave me a really good understanding of the practical trade-offs between different synchronization methods. My partner, Vivian, and I worked on the process-based implementation, while Grayson and Hannah focused on the threaded version. Looking back, I’m really proud of how well our group worked together. We stayed ahead of schedule and managed to turn a pretty challenging set of requirements into a stable, reliable simulation.
Collaboration and Workflow
Working with Vivian was honestly one of the best parts of the project. Instead of dividing the work up and coding separately, we decided early on to use more of a pair-programming style. Most of the time, one of us would be coding while the other talked through the logic, checked synchronization variables, or traced what was happening in shared memory. From the initial `mmap` setup all the way to the final “Scatter” logic, we worked through everything together.
That approach made a huge difference because we were always on the same page. It never felt like one person was carrying more of the workload, and it made debugging much easier since we both understood every part of the codebase.
We also managed our time really well. We started coding on the first day the project was assigned, which let us make steady progress instead of rushing at the end. After every small change, we tested the program to make sure the synchronization was still working correctly. That incremental approach saved us from dealing with huge, hard-to-track bugs later on.
Group Integration
Even though the project was split into two different technical approaches, it never felt like we were working as separate teams. Since we usually worked together during class, the four of us were constantly able to bounce ideas off each other. If Vivian and I got stuck on a synchronization issue, we could compare approaches with Grayson and Hannah and see how they handled something in the threaded version, and they did the same with us.
Because everyone stayed on a similar timeline, the project moved forward smoothly as a group effort rather than four isolated people working independently. Looking back, I think the biggest reason the project went so well was the constant communication and the way we treated lab time as a genuinely collaborative environment. It made the project feel much more manageable and made the overall experience a lot more rewarding.
